Topic 1
Indian Polity & Constitution

Constitutional questions are a staple. LAWCET frequently tests fundamental rights, writs, constitutional bodies, and landmark amendments.

Core Concept

Fundamental Rights — Part III (Arts. 12–35)

  • Art. 14 — Equality before law; Equal protection of laws
  • Art. 19 — Six freedoms: speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession
  • Art. 21 — Right to Life & Personal Liberty (most expansively interpreted)
  • Art. 21A — Right to Education (6–14 years); added by 86th Amendment 2002
  • Art. 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies — Ambedkar called it "heart of the Constitution"

⚡ Constitutional Numbers — Quick Recall

  • Original articles: 395  |  Current: 448
  • Original schedules: 8  |  Current: 12
  • Parts: 25 (originally 22)
  • Emergency: Art. 352 (National), 356 (President's Rule), 360 (Financial)
  • Amendment procedure: Art. 368
  • Abolition of untouchability: Art. 17
How many schedules does the Indian Constitution have?
12 Schedules. Originally 8; schedules were added through amendments. The 9th Schedule (asset protection), 10th (anti-defection), 11th & 12th (Panchayati Raj) were added later.

Type 1
Number & Letter Series
Common Patterns

How to Identify the Pattern

  • Arithmetic: Add/subtract constant (2, 5, 8, 11 → +3 each)
  • Geometric: Multiply/divide by constant (3, 9, 27 → ×3)
  • Squares/Cubes: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25... or 1, 8, 27, 64...
  • Fibonacci-style: Each term = sum of two previous (1,1,2,3,5,8,13...)
  • Alternating series: Split into two interleaved series; solve each separately
  • Difference of differences: Take differences, then differences again to find constant
Worked Example
Find the missing term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ___
(A) 40
(B) 42
(C) 44
(D) 38
Solution: Differences between terms: 4, 6, 8, 10... Each difference increases by 2. Next difference = 12. So: 30 + 12 = 42.
Type 2
Analogies

Identify the relationship in the given pair, then apply the exact same relationship to find the answer.

Word Analogy
Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ___
(A) Book
(B) School
(C) Student
Relationship: Person → Workplace
Number Analogy
9 : 81 :: 7 : ___
(A) 14
(B) 21
(C) 49
Relationship: n : n² → 7² = 49
Type 3
Coding & Decoding
Common Coding Types

Decoding Strategies

  • Letter shift: A→D means +3. Apply same shift to every letter.
  • Reverse alphabet: A=Z, B=Y, C=X (mirror the alphabet)
  • Number coding: A=1, B=2…Z=26 (or reverse: A=26)
  • Symbol/position: Look at position of each letter in given examples
Worked Example
If CAT = FDW, how is DOG coded?
(A) EPH
(B) GRJ
(C) HRI
Solution: C→F (+3), A→D (+3), T→W (+3). Apply: D→G, O→R, G→J = GRJ.
Type 4
Blood Relations
Method Always draw a family tree diagram. Trace each relationship step by step. Never try to solve mentally — even one wrong step cascades.

🔑 Relationship Reference

  • Mother's/Father's son → Brother
  • Mother's/Father's daughter → Sister
  • Mother's brother → Maternal Uncle
  • Father's brother → Paternal Uncle
  • Uncle's/Aunt's child → Cousin
  • Son's wife → Daughter-in-law
  • Wife's/Husband's brother → Brother-in-law
Worked Example
Pointing to a girl, Ram says "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is the girl related to Ram?
(A) Aunt
(B) Sister
(C) Cousin
Solution: Grandfather's only son = Ram's Father. Father's daughter = Ram's Sister.
Type 5
Syllogisms
4-Step Method

How to Solve Syllogisms

  1. Accept both statements as absolute truth.
  2. Draw a Venn diagram covering ALL possible arrangements.
  3. Test each conclusion against your diagram(s).
  4. A conclusion is valid only if it holds in ALL possible diagrams — not just one.
Worked Example
Statements: All cats are animals. All animals are living beings.
Conclusions: I. All cats are living beings.  II. Some living beings are cats.
(A) Only I follows
(B) Both I and II follow
(C) Neither follows
Solution: Cats ⊆ Animals ⊆ Living Beings. So: All cats ARE living beings ✓, and since cats exist, some living beings ARE cats ✓. Both valid.
Type 6
Direction & Distance

🧭 Direction Rules

  • Turning LEFT from North → faces West
  • Turning RIGHT from North → faces East
  • Turning LEFT from East → faces North
  • Distance: use Pythagoras for right-angle movements
  • Sunrise is East → morning shadow falls West
  • Sunset is West → evening shadow falls East
Speed Tip Draw every step on paper — never solve directions mentally. A compass sketch takes 10 seconds and eliminates almost all errors.

Question Type
Principle–Fact (P&F) Method
4-Step Approach

How to Solve Every P&F Question

  1. Read the Principle carefully — note every condition, qualifier, and exception.
  2. Read the Facts — identify the parties, the act, the harm, and the legal issue.
  3. Apply the Principle to Facts — do the facts meet the conditions of the principle?
  4. Choose the answer — pick the option that correctly states the legal outcome. Ignore outside knowledge.
Area 1
Law of Torts

⚖ Core Tort Principles

  • Negligence: Duty of care + Breach + Causation + Damage (all 4 required)
  • Strict Liability (Rylands v Fletcher): Dangerous thing escapes your land → liable regardless of fault
  • Absolute Liability (M.C. Mehta): Indian rule — hazardous enterprise liable absolutely, no exceptions
  • Volenti Non Fit Injuria: Plaintiff voluntarily assumed risk → defendant not liable
  • Vicarious Liability: Master liable for servant's torts in course of employment
  • Defamation: False statement lowering reputation. Libel (written) vs Slander (spoken)
  • Res Ipsa Loquitur: The thing speaks for itself — negligence inferred from the occurrence itself
Principle A person is liable for negligence if they owe a duty of care to another, breach that duty, and the breach causes actual damage to that person.
Facts Dr. Rao operated on Priya and left a surgical sponge inside her body. Priya required a second surgery and suffered complications.
Choose the Correct Option
(A) Not liable — surgery always carries risk.
(B) Not liable — Priya consented to the surgery.
(C) Liable for negligence — Dr. Rao breached his duty of care, causing actual damage.
(D) Liable only if Priya proves intention to leave the sponge.
Explanation: Doctor owes duty of care → leaving a sponge is a clear breach → breach caused actual damage (second surgery, complications). All three elements satisfied. Consent to surgery ≠ consent to negligence. Intent is irrelevant to negligence claims.
Area 2
Contract Law
Indian Contract Act, 1872 — S.10

Essentials of a Valid Contract

  • Offer + Acceptance = Agreement → Agreement + Enforceability = Contract
  • Consideration — something in return (past, present, or future)
  • Free Consent — no coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake
  • Competent Parties — age 18+, sound mind, not disqualified by law
  • Lawful Object — purpose must not be illegal, immoral, or against public policy
Principle An agreement without consideration is void, unless it is made on account of natural love and affection between parties in near relation and is in writing and registered.
Facts Out of love for his sister Meena, Ravi wrote and registered an agreement promising to give her his car as a gift. Ravi later refuses.
Choose the Correct Option
(A) Void — no consideration.
(B) Void — gifts cannot be enforced.
(C) Valid — Meena can enforce it against Ravi.
(D) Voidable at Ravi's option.
Explanation: The principle creates an exception to "no consideration = void." Exception conditions: (1) natural love and affection ✓, (2) near relation (brother-sister) ✓, (3) written ✓, (4) registered ✓. All conditions met → valid and enforceable.
Area 3
Criminal Law — BNS / IPC

⚖ Core Criminal Law Concepts

  • Actus Reus + Mens Rea: Crime = guilty act + guilty mind. No mens rea → generally no crime.
  • Murder vs. CHN: "All murder is culpable homicide; not all culpable homicide is murder." Murder = highest degree of intent.
  • Private Defence: Right to defend body and property. Force must be proportional to threat.
  • Common Intention (S.34 IPC / S.3(5) BNS): Each member of a group with common intention liable for the act.
  • Abetment: Instigating, aiding, or conspiring makes the abettor liable alongside the principal.
  • Triple Talaq: Declared void by Supreme Court 2017; criminalised by Triple Talaq Act 2019.
Principle Voluntarily causing death, or bodily injury likely to cause death, is culpable homicide. If done with the intention of causing death, it is murder.
Facts During an argument, Suresh threw a heavy stone at Ramesh's head. Ramesh died. Suresh says he only intended to scare, not kill.
Choose the Correct Option
(A) Not liable — no intent to kill.
(B) Liable for accidental death only.
(C) Guilty of at least culpable homicide — voluntary act likely to cause death.
(D) Not liable — it arose from an argument.
Explanation: Throwing a heavy stone at someone's head is voluntary and objectively likely to cause death. The principle requires only that the injury was likely to cause death — not a specific intent to kill — for culpable homicide. "Intent to scare" is irrelevant once the act meets the threshold.
