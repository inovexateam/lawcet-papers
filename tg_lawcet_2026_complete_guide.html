Legal Aptitude

Law of TortsMedium
Negligence "DBCD": Duty of care → Breach → Causation → Damage
Donoghue v Stevenson — Neighbour principle; duty of care to all foreseeable persons
Rylands v Fletcher — Strict liability: non-natural use + escape of dangerous thing (exceptions exist)
MC Mehta v UOI — Absolute liability: no exceptions for hazardous industries (stronger than Rylands)
Volenti non fit injuria — Consent is a complete defence to tort
Res ipsa loquitur — Negligence inferred from circumstances ("the thing speaks for itself")
Contributory negligence — Plaintiff's own fault reduces damages
Vicarious liability — Employer liable for employee's tort done in course of employment
Damnum sine injuria — Actual damage but no legal right violated; no remedy
Injuria sine damno — Legal right violated but no actual damage; remedy still exists (Ashby v White)
Indian Contract Act 1872High Priority
Valid Contract "OACCL": Offer + Acceptance + Consideration + Capacity + Lawful object
Sec 2(a) Proposal | 2(b) Promise | 2(e) Agreement | 2(h) Contract
Free consent vitiated by: Coercion (15), Undue influence (16), Fraud (17), Misrepresentation (18), Mistake (20–22)
Void ab initio: minor's contract, unlawful consideration, impossible act
Voidable: contract by coercion/fraud/misrepresentation — option to rescind
Sec 56 — Frustration of contract (impossibility of performance discharges contract)
Sec 73 — Compensation for breach | Sec 74 — Liquidated damages / penalty
Indemnity vs Guarantee: Indemnity = 2 parties, protect from loss | Guarantee = 3 parties (debtor, creditor, surety)
Consideration rule: Must be real but need not be adequate. Past consideration is valid in India (unlike UK).

Principle-Based Questions — StrategyExam Technique
Golden Rule: Apply ONLY the given principle. Ignore your outside legal knowledge completely.
Step 1: Read the principle → identify the key condition/trigger word
Step 2: Extract facts that match or don't match the condition
Step 3: Apply mechanically — if condition met → consequence follows
Trigger words: "only if", "provided that", "unless", "subject to", "notwithstanding"
If principle requires intent — check if facts show intent clearly
Negative questions: "Which is NOT correct?" → eliminate 3 correct options; remaining = answer.

Maths Formulas

Part A — ~8–10 questions from quantitative aptitude. Memorise formulas + tricks.

Percentage & Profit / LossHigh Priority
% change = (New−Old)/Old × 100
Profit% = Profit/CP × 100 | SP (profit) = CP × (100+P%)/100
SP (loss) = CP × (100−L%)/100 | CP from SP = SP × 100/(100+P%)
Trick: Same SP, one at x% profit & one at x% loss → always a LOSS. Loss% = x²/100
Simple & Compound Interest
SI = PRT/100 | CI Amount = P(1+R/100)ᵀ
CI − SI for 2 yrs = P(R/100)² | For 3 yrs = P(R/100)²(3+R/100)
Quick check: 2 yrs at 10% → SI=20%, CI=21%. Difference = SI on first year's interest.
Speed, Distance & Time
Speed = D/T | Avg speed (equal dist) = 2xy/(x+y) (NOT simple average)
Same dir relative speed = |s1−s2| | Opp dir = s1+s2
km/h→m/s: ×5/18 | m/s→km/h: ×18/5
Train crossing platform: (train+platform)/speed
Boats & Streams: Downstream=u+v | Upstream=u−v | Boat speed=(D+U)/2 | Stream speed=(D−U)/2
Number Series, P&C, Probability
AP nth term = a+(n−1)d | Sum = n/2×(2a+(n−1)d)
GP nth term = arⁿ⁻¹
nPr = n!/(n−r)! (order matters) | nCr = n!/r!(n−r)!
Probability = Favourable/Total | P(A or B) = P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)
Series check: Constant diff? (AP) → Constant ratio? (GP) → Diffs form series? → Squares/cubes?
EJOTY: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25 — instant letter position lookup for coding questions!
Geometry & Clocks
Circle: A=πr², P=2πr
Triangle: A=½bh | Equil: √3/4 a²
Rectangle: lb | Trapezium: ½(a+b)h
Pythagoras: a²+b²=c² | Triplets: 3-4-5, 5-12-13
Cylinder vol: πr²h | Sphere: 4/3πr³
Clock angle = |30H−5.5M| degrees
Min hand: 6°/min | Hour hand: 0.5°/min
Hands coincide every 65⁵⁄₁₁ minutes
Alligation: (d−m):(m−c)

Error Spotting — Common TrapsHigh Priority
Neither/Either → singular verb: "Neither of them is correct"
Less vs Fewer: Less = uncountable | Fewer = countable: "Fewer lawyers, less justice"
Between vs Among: Between = 2 people/things | Among = 3 or more
Lay vs Lie: Lay = to place (transitive) | Lie = to recline (intransitive)
Each/Every/None/Anyone → always take singular verb
Who/Whom: Who = subject pronoun | Whom = object pronoun ("To whom it may concern")
One of the + plural noun → singular verb: "One of the judges WAS present"
Neither A nor B → verb agrees with B (nearest): "Neither the lawyer nor the witnesses WERE present"
FANBOYS (coordinating conjunctions): For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

Coding-DecodingHigh Priority
EJOTY trick: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25 → instantly know any letter's position!
Opposite letter pairs: A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X... (pair sum always = 27)
Letter positions: A=1, B=2... Z=26 | Reverse: A=26, Z=1
Step 1: Identify shift direction (+, −, opposite) | Step 2: Verify on 2–3 letters before applying

Blood RelationsHigh Priority
Golden Rule: DRAW a family tree for every question. Never solve in your head.

Directions & SyllogismsMedium
Compass: "Never Eat Stale Wheat" → N, E, S, W (clockwise)
Facing North: left=West, right=East | Facing South: left=East, right=West (reversed!)
Sunrise=East; shadow points West | Sunset=West; shadow points East
Syllogism rules: All+All=All | All+No=No | Some+All=Some | Some+No=Some Not
"All A are B" → Some B are A (true) but NOT "All B are A"
"No A is B" → "No B is A" (always true — reversible)
Draw Venn diagrams for every syllogism. Pictorial approach never fails.
